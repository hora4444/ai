# Introduction

Mathematical induction is a powerful tool used in mathematics to prove statements over an infinite sequence of natural numbers, which are often denoted as \(\mathbb{N}\). This technique relies on two key principles: the base case and the induction step.

## Definition of Mathematical Induction

### Base Case
The first principle involves establishing that the statement is true for a specific starting value or set of values. For example:

$$P(1) = True$$

This means our initial claim \( P \), which will be tested, holds when \( n=1 \).

### Inductive Step (Assumption)
Once we have verified the base case (\( P(n_0) \)), the next step is to assume that for some integer or natural number \( k > 1 \):

$$P(k) = True$$

This serves as our "assumption" about which all further steps are based.

### Inductive Conclusion
Finally, we aim to prove that:

$$P(n + 1)$$ holds true when given the assumption of \( P(k) \). This step is crucial because it demonstrates consistency and correctness in extending from previous known cases (\( n = k \)) through a natural progression into new values.

### Proving Inductive Conclusion
To establish this, we must show that:

- **Adding one** to our "assumed true" case leads directly back to the original statement \( P(n) \).

$$P(k+1)\Rightarrow P(n_0)$$

This essentially means adding 1 and showing it's equivalent or returns us right where we started.
